## Role in value chains

Indonesia produced around 60% of the world's mined nickel in 2024 and has become the leading source of growth in refined nickel supply. Much of its output is processed in large industrial parks, notably in Central Sulawesi and North Maluku, into products for stainless steel and, increasingly, battery materials. Cobalt is recovered as a by-product of some nickel processing.

## Major policy drivers

- **An ore export ban.** Since 1 January 2020 Indonesia has prohibited nickel ore exports, to require processing at home. This is part of a wider policy of downstream industrialisation.
- **Energy transition finance.** The Just Energy Transition Partnership, announced in 2022 with an initial US$20 billion package, addresses the power sector. How it treats power supply for industrial parks matters for nickel's emissions and for local air quality.
- **Transparency.** Indonesia implements the Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative standard.

## Affected people and places

People affected include residents of villages near mines and processing parks, fishing communities along nearby coasts, Indigenous communities in forested mining areas, and large numbers of workers, many of them migrants from other provinces or abroad, living in and around industrial parks.

## Decision-making institutions

- **Ministry of Energy and Mineral Resources:** mining licences and mineral export rules.
- **Investment and industry authorities:** approvals for processing investment and industrial estates.
- **Provincial and district governments:** spatial planning, local permits and services.
- **Industrial park operators and tenant companies:** day-to-day site management, safety systems and worker housing.

## Worker and environmental issues

On 24 December 2023, a furnace accident at a nickel smelter in the Indonesia Morowali Industrial Park killed workers and injured dozens. Initial company figures reported 13 deaths, and later reporting raised the toll to around 20. The park operator revised its initial account of the cause, and labour groups criticised safety and evacuation arrangements. The accident raises questions that apply well beyond one site:

- Who is responsible for safety where many contractors share a park?
- Are workers able to raise concerns and organise without retaliation?
- Are emergency plans and evacuation routes in place and practised?

Environmental questions include deforestation for mining, sediment run-off to coastal waters, the storage of processing residues, and air quality near power plants and smelters. The Just Energy Transition Partnership is a programme-level package whose headline figure combines government pledges and private-finance mobilisation goals. It is not an open grant facility. See the [finance records](../../finance/indonesia-jetp/) for verification status.

## What it is

Copper is a reddish metal that conducts electricity and heat exceptionally well, resists corrosion and can be drawn into wire. It is mined mostly from sulphide and oxide ores that typically contain a small percentage of copper or less.

## How it is used

Grids, building wiring, motors and electronics account for most copper use. Electric vehicles use more copper than conventional cars, and renewable power and grid expansion use large amounts. Aluminium can substitute in some cables, with trade-offs in size and performance.

## Lifecycle and geography

Ore is crushed and concentrated at or near the mine, then smelted and refined into high-purity cathode, or processed by leaching and electrowinning. Concentrate is often shipped across borders for smelting. Copper appears on India's 2023 critical minerals list. Across the major energy minerals, refining is more concentrated than mining, and copper is part of that pattern.

## Development questions

- **Water:** Many large copper regions are arid. Who has priority when mines and communities draw on the same water?
- **Tailings:** Copper mining creates very large volumes of waste. Who is responsible for tailings dams over decades, including after closure?
- **Smelter emissions:** Smelting can release sulphur dioxide and metals if poorly controlled. What monitoring is public?
- **Value:** Is copper exported as concentrate, or processed in the country? Who captures the margin?

## Environment and labour

The main risks include land disturbance, water depletion and contamination, tailings failure, and occupational hazards in mines and smelters, including dust, heat and confined spaces.

## Efficiency, reuse, substitution and recycling

Copper can be recycled repeatedly without losing its properties, and scrap is already an important part of supply. Recovery rates are lower for copper in small electronics than in large cables and buildings. Designing products for disassembly, and collecting electronic waste safely, raises recovery and protects the people who dismantle it.

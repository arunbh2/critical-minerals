## What it is

Graphite is a form of carbon. **Natural graphite** is mined, often as flakes, and then purified and shaped. **Synthetic graphite** is manufactured from petroleum or coal by-products at very high temperatures, which uses a lot of energy.

## How it is used

Most lithium-ion battery anodes use graphite, natural, synthetic or blended. Graphite is also used in steelmaking electrodes, refractories and lubricants.

## Lifecycle and geography

Battery-grade graphite requires purification and shaping into spheres, followed by coating. This processing is highly concentrated: the IEA identifies China as the dominant source of recent supply growth. China introduced export permits for certain graphite products from December 2023. Graphite is on India's 2023 critical minerals list.

## Development questions

- **Purification:** Chemical purification of natural graphite can involve strong acids. What controls protect workers and water?
- **Energy:** Synthetic graphite production is energy-intensive. What powers it?
- **Value:** Do producing countries export raw concentrate, or build anode processing?

## Environment and labour

The main risks include dust from mining and processing, chemical handling during purification, and emissions from the energy used for synthetic graphite.

## Efficiency, reuse, substitution and recycling

Graphite recovery from spent batteries is less established than recovery of cathode metals. Silicon additions and other anode materials may change graphite needs. Neither should be assumed to reduce demand quickly.

## Why it matters

Every tonne of material that is reused or recovered is a tonne that need not be mined. But "circular" is not automatically safe or fair. Battery recycling can expose workers to fires and toxic fumes. Lead-acid battery recycling, in particular, is a well-known source of serious exposure where it is done informally.

## The levers, in order

1. **Use less:** material efficiency and system choices.
2. **Use longer:** durable design, repair and maintenance.
3. **Use again:** refurbishment and second-life applications.
4. **Recover:** collection and recycling.
5. **Substitute:** different materials or chemistries where they reduce harm overall.

## Policy is moving

The EU Batteries Regulation sets recovery and recycled-content targets and requires digital battery passports for larger batteries from 2027. India's Battery Waste Management Rules, notified in 2022 and since amended, introduced extended producer responsibility. Rules like these create markets and obligations. Who benefits depends on how collectors, repairers and recyclers are included.

## The livelihood question

In many countries informal workers already collect and dismantle electronics and batteries. Formal schemes can offer safer, better-paid work, or squeeze these workers out. Ask who was consulted in designing the scheme, and what happens to people who earned a living from the old system.

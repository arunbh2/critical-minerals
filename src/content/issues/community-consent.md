## Why it matters

Decisions about exploration, licensing and project design shape everything that follows: land use, water, jobs and closure. If people who live with the consequences are excluded at the start, later benefit schemes rarely repair the loss of trust.

## What good practice requires

- **Timing:** Information and engagement before rights are granted, not after.
- **Accessibility:** Local languages, formats that work for people with limited literacy, and meetings people can actually attend, including women, young people and marginalised groups.
- **Influence:** Engagement that can change decisions, including the option of a project not going ahead.
- **Free, prior and informed consent (FPIC):** For Indigenous peoples, a standard recognised in international instruments. Consent must be given freely, before decisions, with full information. Many companies and financiers have adopted FPIC in their standards.
- **Representation:** Clarity about who speaks for whom, and safeguards against capture by a few individuals.

## Signals to look for

Was a project's information public before rights were allocated? Were dissenting views recorded? Did anything change as a result of consultation? Is there an agreement, and can the community enforce it?

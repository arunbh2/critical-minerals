## Why it matters

Minerals are finite. The value they generate, through profits, wages, taxes, royalties and local business, is the main lasting benefit a producing place can keep. How that value is shared decides whether mining leaves behind skills, infrastructure and services, or depletion and grievance.

## Channels of value

- **Fiscal:** royalties, taxes, dividends from state-owned firms.
- **Employment:** direct and contracted jobs, and their quality.
- **Local enterprise:** procurement of goods and services from local businesses.
- **Processing:** adding value in the country, as Indonesia's nickel ore export ban since 2020 seeks to do.
- **Earmarked funds:** for example India's District Mineral Foundations, financed by levies on mining leaseholders, which had collected more than ₹1 lakh crore by early 2025.
- **Agreements:** benefit-sharing or community development agreements.

## The distribution question

Value can be captured nationally but not locally, or locally by a few. Look at who decides spending, whether affected people participate, whether spending is published, and whether services improve.

## Benefits are not a substitute

Benefit sharing does not replace rights. Payment cannot buy out a community's right to be consulted, a worker's right to safety, or anyone's right to remedy.

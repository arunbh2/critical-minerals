"Just transition" means moving to a low-carbon economy in a way that is fair to workers and communities, especially those whose lives and livelihoods are most affected. In minerals, that fairness has to hold in places that produce materials, not only in places that consume clean technologies.

The UN Secretary-General's Panel on Critical Energy Transition Minerals, reporting in September 2024, put it plainly: the first of its seven voluntary principles is that human rights must be at the core of all mineral value chains.

## Rights first

Some things are not benefits. They are baseline obligations:

- **Participation and consent.** Meaningful information, early, in an accessible language. Real influence over decisions. For Indigenous peoples, respect for free, prior and informed consent as recognised in international standards.
- **Safe and decent work.** Safe workplaces, fair pay, freedom to organise, and protection that extends to contractors and subcontractors.
- **A healthy environment.** Clean water and air, safe waste storage, and planning for closure.
- **Access to remedy.** A way to raise grievances and obtain redress, including through courts or independent bodies, without retaliation.

A project cannot offset a rights violation with jobs or investment. That is why this portal's assessment tool gives no score.

## What meaningful benefit looks like

Beyond rights, benefits can be meaningful when they are:

- **Decided with the people affected**, not for them.
- **Distributed fairly**, including to women, informal workers, and people who lose access to land or water.
- **Lasting**, building skills, local enterprise, infrastructure and public services that outlive the mine.
- **Transparent and accountable**, with published commitments, budgets and results.
- **Financed reliably**, through mechanisms that do not depend on goodwill.

## Testing claims of benefit

When a project or policy claims to benefit communities, test the claim in three layers:

1. **Commitment:** What exactly was promised, by whom, and is it written down?
2. **Implementation:** What has actually been done, spent or delivered?
3. **Outcome:** What changed for people, as measured and as experienced, and who checked?

Collected money is a commitment-level fact. Analyses of India's District Mineral Foundations, for example, reported that roughly half of cumulative collections had not been spent. Collection alone does not show benefit.
